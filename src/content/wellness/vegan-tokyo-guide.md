Tokyo is legendary for its food scene, but traditional broth (dashi) contains fish flakes. Fortunately, a massive wave of dedicated vegan ramen, sushi, and macrobiotic dining centers has transformed the metropolis.

## Quick Reference Summary

| Question | Direct Answer |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Is dashi vegetarian?** | No, traditional dashi is made with skipjack tuna (katsuobushi) and kelp. Request kombu-dashi (kelp-only). |
| **Where is the best vegan ramen?** | T's TanTan (inside Tokyo Station) and Kyushu Jangara (Harajuku) offer outstanding plant-based ramen. |
| **What is Shojin Ryori?** | Traditional Buddhist temple cuisine that is entirely vegan, organic, and seasonal. |

## The Rise of Vegan Ramen

Ramen is typically made with heavy pork or chicken bone broths. However, Tokyo's top vegan spots use sesame paste, soy milk, and vegetable broths to create rich, satisfying bowls.

> "Tokyo Station's T's TanTan is a culinary pilgrimage site. Their spicy sesame noodles prove that flavor does not require animals." — Akhi

### Top Tokyo Vegan Dining Recommendations:
- **T's TanTan**: Famous for TanTan noodles and vegan gyoza. Located inside the gates of Tokyo Station.
- **Shojin Ryori (Buddhist Temple Dining)**: Try it near Mount Takao or inside temples in Kamakura for a slow, ritualistic meal.
- **Ain Soph. Ripple**: Located in Kabukicho, Harajuku, specializing in vegan burgers and comfort foods.
