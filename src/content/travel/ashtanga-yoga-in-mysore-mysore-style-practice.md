For dedicated yogis around the world, **Ashtanga Yoga in Mysore** represents the ultimate pilgrimage. It is here, in the quiet residential neighborhood of Gokulam, that the dynamic system of Ashtanga Vinyasa Yoga was perfected under Sri K. Pattabhi Jois and continues today through his grandson Paramaguru Sharath Jois, along with respected lineage teachers.

Practicing Ashtanga in its birthplace is fundamentally different from attending a modern gym or studio class. It is an immersion in *Parampara*—the direct, unbroken transmission of knowledge from master to disciple through daily, disciplined self-practice.

This guide provides a comprehensive roadmap for practitioners seeking to understand, prepare for, and immerse themselves in traditional **Mysore-style Ashtanga Yoga**.

> **Quick Tip:** Maintain strict consistency in your practice start time each morning. Waking and practicing at the exact same hour daily stabilizes your circadian rhythm and nervous system response.

## The Core Pillars of Ashtanga Yoga (*Tristhana*)

At the heart of Ashtanga Yoga lies **Tristhana**—the simultaneous integration of three focal points during practice:

| Pillar of Tristhana | Sanskrit Name | Physical & Mental Mechanism | Purpose & Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Posture** | *Asana* | Precise mechanical alignment across fixed series | Builds physical strength, flexibility, & structural integrity |
| **2. Breath & Locks** | *Pranayama & Bandhas* | *Ujjayi* breath synchronized with *Mula* & *Uddiyana Bandhas* | Purifies internal organs, builds internal heat (*Tapas*), & stabilizes energy |
| **3. Focal Gaze Point** | *Drishti* | Directing eye gaze to 9 specific points (e.g. nose, third eye, toes) | Draws attention inward (*Pratyahara*) & stops mental distraction |

## Understanding the Ashtanga Series Architecture

The Ashtanga system is organized into six progressive series of postures:

### #1- Primary Series (*Yoga Chikitsa* / Yoga Therapy)
The Primary Series consists of approximately 75 postures designed to realign the spine, detoxify the digestive tract, and build foundational strength and stamina. It includes Sun Salutations, Standing Postures, Seated Forward Bends, Spinal Twists, and Closing Inversions.

### #2- Intermediate Series (*Nadi Shodhana* / Nerve Cleansing)
The Intermediate Series focuses on purifying the nervous system and subtle energy channels (*Nadis*). It introduces dynamic backbends, deep hip openers, arm balances, and headstand variations.

### #3- Advanced Series A, B, C, D (*Sthira Bhaga* / Divine Stability)
The Advanced Series demands extraordinary strength, flexibility, and mental stability. It is practiced by senior, long-term practitioners under direct master supervision.

## How to Register & Practice at Sharath Yoga Centre (SYC)

The **Sharath Yoga Centre (SYC)** in Gokulam is the recognized global headquarters for Ashtanga practice. Here are the step-by-step registration requirements:

### Step 1: Online Application Window
SYC opens registration on its official website several months prior to each season. Slots for 1-month to 2-month study blocks open on specific dates and fill up within minutes.

### Step 2: Documentation Requirements
Applicants must submit a passport copy, recent photograph, and proof of prior Ashtanga study under an authorized or certified teacher.

### Step 3: Arrival & Shala Orientation
Upon arrival in Gokulam, students complete in-person registration, receive an official shala ID card, and are assigned a specific early morning batch start time (ranging from 4:30 AM to 8:00 AM).

## Essential Mysore-Style Etiquette Rules

1. **Quiet Arrival**: Enter the shala silently. Lay your cotton mat rug down softly without snapping or shaking it near neighboring practitioners.
2. **Wait for Posture Catching**: Do not move on to a new posture in the series until your teacher explicitly teaches it to you ("catches" you in the pose).
3. **No Water Drinking During Practice**: Internal heat (*Agni*) is built through continuous breath and posture. Drinking water during practice extinguishes this internal fire. Hydrate thoroughly after your practice is complete.
4. **Observe Moon Days & Ladies' Rest**: Do not practice on Full Moon or New Moon days. Women take 3 days of rest during heavy menstrual days to respect internal energy flows.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between Ashtanga Yoga and Vinyasa Flow?
Ashtanga Yoga follows a fixed, unchanging sequence of postures practiced self-paced every day. Modern Vinyasa Flow uses creative, changing sequences set by an instructor.

### How long does a full Primary Series practice take?
A full Primary Series practice typically takes between 75 and 90 minutes, concluding with 10 to 15 minutes of deep final relaxation (*Savasana*).

### Can I practice Ashtanga in Mysore if I have injuries?
Yes. Experienced Mysore teachers excel at adapting postures using props, altered angles, or temporary posture skips to allow your body to heal while maintaining daily breath discipline.
