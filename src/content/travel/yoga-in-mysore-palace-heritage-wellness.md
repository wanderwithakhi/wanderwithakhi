The relationship between **yoga and Mysore Palace** is one of the most remarkable chapters in modern cultural history. Long before international practitioners flooded the cafes of Gokulam, modern physical yoga was revived, refined, and codified within the opulent halls and manicured grounds of Amba Vilas Palace (commonly known as Mysore Palace).

Under the enlightened patronage of Maharaja Krishnaraja Wadiyar IV during the 1930s, Mysore became the world epicenter of yogic experimentation. Understanding this royal heritage illuminates why Mysore remains the undeniable spiritual home for millions of practitioners across the world today.

## The Visionary Patronage of Maharaja Krishnaraja Wadiyar IV

To understand how Mysore became synonymous with yoga, one must appreciate the vision of Maharaja Krishnaraja Wadiyar IV (1884–1940). Ruling the Kingdom of Mysore with exceptional administrative insight, he sought to modernize his state while preserving traditional Indian classical arts, music, Sanskrit scholarship, and physical health.

Recognizing that traditional yogic practices were fading into obscurity under British colonial rule, the Maharaja sought a master who could restore yoga as a vibrant, scientific discipline. In Sri Tirumalai Krishnamacharya, he found an extraordinary scholar holding degrees in all six classical systems of Indian philosophy, alongside deep mastery of Hatha Yoga and Ayurveda.

## Inside the Palace Yogashala: How Modern Vinyasa Was Born

In 1933, the Maharaja provided dedicated space and funding for Krishnamacharya to run an official **Yogashala** inside the palace complex (and later at the nearby Jaganmohan Palace). 

### #1- Dynamic Sequencing for Young Scholars
The Palace Yogashala primarily taught young boys enrolled in the Royal Sanskrit College. To engage these energetic youths, Krishnamacharya synthesized ancient texts like the *Yoga Korunta* with traditional Indian wrestling drills (*mallakhamb*) and Western gymnastics. The result was dynamic **Vinyasa**: linking fluid breath with continuous, energetic movement between postures.

### #2- The Birth of Individualized Yoga Therapeutics
In addition to teaching dynamic youth groups, Krishnamacharya served as the private yoga therapist to the royal family. He adjusted practices based on age, body constitution, and medical conditions, creating the foundation for modern personalized yoga therapy (*Viniyoga*).

## The Four Pillars: Legendary Students of the Mysore Palace

The legacy of the Mysore Palace Yogashala lives on through four iconic disciples trained directly by Krishnamacharya:

### #1- K. Pattabhi Jois (1915–2009)
Pattabhi Jois studied at the Palace Yogashala for over two decades. He codified the dynamic sequences taught by Krishnamacharya into the **Ashtanga Yoga Primary, Intermediate, and Advanced Series**, establishing the Ashtanga Yoga Research Institute in Mysore in 1948.

### #2- B.K.S. Iyengar (1918–2007)
As Krishnamacharya’s brother-in-law, a young, frail B.K.S. Iyengar began his training at Mysore Palace. He went on to revolutionize yoga through anatomical alignment, precision, and the creative use of props (blocks, straps, and bolsters), detailing his system in the landmark book *Light on Yoga*.

### #3- Indra Devi (1899–2002)
Indra Devi made history as the first foreign woman admitted to study at the Mysore Palace Yogashala. Under Krishnamacharya's guidance, she learned yogic posture and breathwork, later introducing yoga to Hollywood celebrities and spreading the practice throughout Latin America and the United States.

### #4- T.K.V. Desikachar (1938–2016)
Krishnamacharya’s son, Desikachar, studied deeply with his father in Mysore and Chennai, emphasizing the therapeutic application of breath, chanting, and posture tailored to each unique individual.

## Exploring Mysore Palace & Heritage Sites Today

While daily shala practice takes place in Gokulam today, visiting the historic royal sites offers a profound connection to yoga's roots:

- **Amba Vilas Palace (Mysore Palace)**: Tour the grand Durbar Hall, intricate rosewood doorways, and stunning stained glass ceilings. 
- **Jaganmohan Palace & Art Gallery**: Located just west of the main palace, this historic building housed the original Yogashala demonstrations in the 1930s. Today it displays exquisite royal paintings by Raja Ravi Varma.
- **Chamundi Hill & Chamundeshwari Temple**: Perched atop Chamundi Hill overlooking the city, climbing the 1,000 stone steps at sunrise is a favorite weekend pilgrimage for local yogis.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I practice yoga on the grounds of Mysore Palace?
While daily personal practice is held in private shalas, the Mysuru Palace board hosts massive public celebrations on June 21st for **International Day of Yoga**, welcoming tens of thousands of practitioners to perform sun salutations in front of the Palace.

### Where is the original Jaganmohan Palace Yogashala located?
Jaganmohan Palace is situated near the city center, approximately 10 minutes by auto-rickshaw from Gokulam. It is now open to the public as an art museum.

### How did Mysore Palace influence modern yoga styles like Vinyasa Flow?
Virtually all modern Vinyasa, Power, Ashtanga, and Iyengar Yoga styles trace their direct lineage back to the teaching methods developed by T. Krishnamacharya inside the Mysore Palace during the 1930s.
