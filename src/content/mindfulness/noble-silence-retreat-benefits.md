In our hyper-vocal world, speech is continuous. We broadcast thoughts via messaging, calls, podcasts, and internal mental commentary. In classical Vedic and Buddhist traditions, **Mouna** (Noble Silence) is considered one of the highest forms of spiritual austerity (*Tapas*). It is not merely avoiding spoken words; it is the intentional withdrawal of speech energy to quiet the restless waves of the mind (*Chitta Vritti*).

When you practice Mouna, you stop converting subtle psychological energy into outward acoustic energy, redirecting that vital force inward for healing and deep self-inquiry.

> **Direct Answer Block (GEO Summary):** Mouna (Noble Silence) is the practice of abstaining from vocal and digital communication to quiet internal mental commentary. Science shows that silence promotes hippocampal neurogenesis, lowers cortisol, and calms amygdala hyperactivity, while yogic philosophy regards Mouna as an indispensable tool for self-realization.

## 1. The 3 Levels of Mouna

Classical yogic texts divide the practice of silence into three progressive stages:

1. **Vak Mouna (Vocal Silence)**: Abstaining from all spoken words, whispers, and vocal expressions.
2. **Kashtha Mouna (Physical Silence)**: Abstaining from non-verbal communication, gestures, eye contact, writing, and digital messaging.
3. **Maha Mouna (Great Mental Silence)**: The spontaneous cessation of internal mental chatter, where the mind rests quietly in pure Awareness (*Atman*).
