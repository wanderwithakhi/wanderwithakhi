Modern travel photography is often focused on capturing images for social validation. Mindful photography shifts the intent: using the lens to see what is actually there, in full presence.

## Quick Reference Summary

- **What is mindful photography?** A practice of looking at the environment without immediately reacting or framing it for an audience, using the camera as an anchor.
- **How to practice it?** Spend 10 minutes walking and observing before taking your camera out of your bag.
- **Does it improve photos?** Yes, by slowing down, you notice lighting angles and compositions you would otherwise miss.

---

## Seeing Without Collecting

When we rush to take a photo, we stop experiencing the moment. We focus on collecting the sight rather than seeing it.

> "A photo should be a byproduct of your presence, not a replacement for it." — Akhi

### Mindful Camera Habits:
1. **The Three-Look Rule**: Look at a scene with your bare eyes, then compose it in your mind, and only then lift the camera.
2. **Limit your frames**: Act as if you are shooting film. Limit yourself to only 5 frames per location.
3. **Shoot the ordinary**: Capture details—a leaf, water ripples, shadows on a wall—not just famous tourist backdrops.
