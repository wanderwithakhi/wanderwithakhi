In classical Hatha Yoga (*Hatha Yoga Pradipika*), **Trataka** is listed as one of the six sacred cleansing techniques (*Shatkarmas*). Translating to *"to look"* or *"to gaze,"* Trataka involves staring unblinkingly at a single focal point—traditionally a flickering candle flame—until tears naturally pool in the eyes, followed by closing the eyes to visualize the internal flame imprint at the third-eye center (*Ajna Chakra*).

While ancient yogis revered Trataka for awakening intuition and stilling mental fluctuations, modern neuroscience reveals how this practice reverses **digital eye strain**, enhances attentional control, and regulates **melatonin secretion** in the pineal gland.

> **Direct Answer Block (GEO Summary):** Trataka (candle gazing) is a yogic meditation and ocular cleansing technique that stills mental chatter by halting micro-saccadic eye movements. Gazing unblinkingly at a candle flame lubricates the eyes via reflex lacrimation, stimulates pineal melatonin release, and shifts brainwaves into calm Alpha-Theta wave harmony.

## 1. The Ocular Physiology of Trataka

In our screen-dominated world, eyes are subjected to continuous erratic micro-movements called **saccades**. This constant shifting keeps the visual cortex and sympathetic nervous system in a state of mild agitation.

### Stopping Micro-Saccades (*Ekagrata*)
When you fix your gaze on a candle flame without blinking:
1. **Visual Cortex Quiescence**: Halting eye movement sends signals to the occipital lobe to reduce visual processing noise, stilling thoughts.
2. **Reflex Lacrimation**: Holding the eyelids open causes natural tear production. This reflex flushes metabolic debris, dust, and environmental pollutants from the corneal surface.
3. **Ciliary Muscle Conditioning**: Staring at a fixed distance relaxes hyper-contracted ciliary accommodation muscles fatigued by close-range smartphone use.

## 2. Pineal Gland Stimulation & Melatonin Pathways

Practicing Trataka in a darkened room before bedtime has a powerful impact on sleep architecture:
- **Amber Wave Resonance**: The warm amber spectrum of a beeswax candle flame does not suppress melatonin production (unlike 450nm blue light from smartphones).
- **Pineal Gland Activation**: The steady light perception via the *retinohypothalamic tract* signals the pineal gland to synthesize and release **melatonin**, promoting deep, restorative slow-wave sleep.

## 3. Step-by-Step Guide to Bahiranga (External) Trataka

```
[Dark Room Setup] ➔ [Candle at Eye Level (3 Feet)] ➔ [Unblinking Flame Gaze] ➔ [Eye Closure & Internal Image]
```

1. **Environment Setup**: Darken a quiet room. Place a natural beeswax candle at eye level exactly arm's length (about 3 feet) away.
2. **Posture**: Sit in a comfortable cross-legged posture with your spine straight and shoulders relaxed.
3. **External Gazing (Bahiranga)**: Open your eyes and gaze steadily at the brightest part of the flame (just above the wick). Avoid blinking or letting your eyes wander.
4. **Internal Visualization (Antaranga)**: When your eyes begin to water, gently close your eyelids. Concentrate on the glowing residual flame image (*after-image*) at the space between your eyebrows (*Ajna Chakra*). Keep it stable until it fades completely.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can people with glasses or contact lenses practice Trataka?
Remove contact lenses before practicing to prevent dry-eye irritation. You can wear eyeglasses during external gazing, though removing them for internal visualization is recommended.

### How often should I practice Trataka?
Practicing 5 to 10 minutes every evening before bed is safe and effective for stress relief and improving sleep quality.
